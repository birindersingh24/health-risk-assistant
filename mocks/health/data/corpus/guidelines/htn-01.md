# Blood pressure measurement and staging

> **Mock document for a software demo. Not clinical guidance; do not use for patient care.**
> Example Clinic Protocols, Primary Care. This version replaces htn-01-v1 (2019).

## Taking a reliable reading

Seat the patient with back supported, feet flat and arm resting at heart level. Wait five minutes before the first reading. Use a cuff that fits the arm. Take at least two readings one to two minutes apart and record the average.

One high clinic reading does not establish hypertension. Confirm with readings at a second visit, or with home or ambulatory measurements, before starting treatment, unless the reading is in the severe range (htn-03).

## Categories

| Category | Systolic (mmHg) | | Diastolic (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | below 120 | and | below 80 |
| Elevated | 120 to 129 | and | below 80 |
| Stage 1 hypertension | 130 to 139 | or | 80 to 89 |
| Stage 2 hypertension | 140 or higher | or | 90 or higher |
| Severe range | 180 or higher | and/or | 120 or higher |

When systolic and diastolic fall in different categories, use the higher one. For example, 129/79 is elevated, because 129 is in the elevated band and 79 is below 80. A reading of 129/80 is stage 1, because the diastolic value reaches 80.

## What each category means for care

- Normal: recheck once a year.
- Elevated: lifestyle advice; recheck in three to six months. This is not hypertension and should not be coded as I10.
- Stage 1 and stage 2: confirm the reading, then follow htn-02 for treatment.
- Severe range: follow htn-03 the same day.

## Reading treated patients

For patients already on treatment, the categories describe control rather than diagnosis. A treated patient above 130/80 is "not at target". Use the mean of the last three visits to judge control, not a single reading.
