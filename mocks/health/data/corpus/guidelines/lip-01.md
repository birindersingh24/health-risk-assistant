# Lipid testing and statin eligibility

> **Mock document for a software demo. Not clinical guidance; do not use for patient care.**
> Example Clinic Protocols, Primary Care.

## When to test

Adults aged 20 and over need a lipid panel (total cholesterol, HDL, LDL and triglycerides) at least every five years. Test once a year for patients on a statin, with diabetes, or with chronic kidney disease. After starting or changing a statin, recheck in 4 to 12 weeks.

A patient with no lipid panel in the last five years is overdue. A risk score calculated from an overdue panel is unreliable: say so, and order a new panel before making a statin decision.

## Who should be offered a statin

Work down this list; the first line that applies decides.

1. LDL cholesterol 190 mg/dL or higher: high-intensity statin, whatever the risk score.
2. Diabetes, age 40 to 75: at least a moderate-intensity statin; high intensity if the risk band is high.
3. Risk band high (risk-01): recommend a statin, usually moderate or high intensity.
4. Risk band intermediate: discuss a statin. Factors in favor include a family history of premature heart disease, LDL 160 or higher, chronic kidney disease, and a persistently raised blood pressure.
5. Risk band low or borderline: lifestyle advice; recalculate the score at the next panel.

A patient in group 1, 2 or 3 who is not taking a statin has a care gap, unless the notes record that a statin was offered and declined, or that the patient cannot tolerate one. In those cases, follow ops-02 instead of flagging a gap.

## Statin intensity

| Intensity | Examples (daily dose) | Expected LDL reduction |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate | atorvastatin 10 to 20 mg, rosuvastatin 5 to 10 mg | about 30 to 49% |
| High | atorvastatin 40 to 80 mg, rosuvastatin 20 to 40 mg | about 50% or more |

## Monitoring on treatment

Recheck lipids 4 to 12 weeks after starting, then once a year. Ask about muscle symptoms at each review; see lip-02 for how to handle them. Do not stop a statin because of mild symptoms without a review.
