# Diabetes screening and diagnosis

> **Mock document for a software demo. Not clinical guidance; do not use for patient care.**
> Example Clinic Protocols, Primary Care.

## Who to screen

Screen every adult from age 35 for type 2 diabetes. Screen earlier, at any adult age, when the patient has a body mass index of 25 or more together with at least one other risk factor. Risk factors in this clinic are a first-degree relative with diabetes, treated or untreated hypertension, HDL cholesterol below 35 mg/dL, a history of gestational diabetes, and physical inactivity.

Use HbA1c as the default screening test because it needs no fasting. A fasting plasma glucose is an acceptable alternative.

If the result is normal, repeat screening every three years. If the result is in the prediabetes range, follow protocol dm-02, which moves testing to once a year.

## Diagnostic thresholds

| Category | HbA1c | Fasting plasma glucose |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | below 5.7% | below 100 mg/dL |
| Prediabetes | 5.7% to 6.4% | 100 to 125 mg/dL |
| Diabetes range | 6.5% or higher | 126 mg/dL or higher |

The boundaries are inclusive exactly as written. An HbA1c of 6.4% is prediabetes, not diabetes. An HbA1c of 6.5% is in the diabetes range. A fasting glucose of 125 mg/dL is prediabetes; 126 mg/dL is in the diabetes range.

Round HbA1c to one decimal place before comparing it with the table. Do not average results from different dates to decide the category; use each result on its own.

## Confirming a diagnosis

One result in the diabetes range is not enough to diagnose diabetes in a patient without classic symptoms. The diagnosis needs two abnormal results, either:

- the same test abnormal on two different dates, or
- two different tests abnormal from the same sample or the same visit.

When a patient already has two HbA1c results of 6.5% or more on different dates, the diagnosis is confirmed. The clinician should add type 2 diabetes (ICD-10-CM E11.x) to the problem list, discuss the result with the patient, and start the diabetes care plan in dm-03. A referral to the diabetes educator follows the routes in ops-01.

A confirmed result that is not on the problem list is a documentation gap. Protocol dq-01 describes how to flag it.

## When HbA1c can mislead

HbA1c reflects average glucose over about three months and can be wrong when red blood cell turnover is abnormal. Prefer glucose-based tests, or confirm with one, when the patient has anemia, has had a recent blood transfusion or significant blood loss, is pregnant, or has advanced kidney disease (eGFR below 30). Say so in the note when you rely on glucose for this reason.
