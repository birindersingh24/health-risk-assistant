# Metformin and kidney function

> **Mock document for a software demo. Not clinical guidance; do not use for patient care.**
> Example Clinic Protocols, Primary Care.

## Why kidney function matters

Metformin is cleared by the kidneys. When kidney function falls, metformin builds up and the risk of a rare but serious complication, lactic acidosis, rises. The dose limit therefore follows the latest eGFR.

## Dose by eGFR

| Latest eGFR | Starting metformin | Already taking metformin |
|---|---|---|
| 60 or higher | allowed | no change; check eGFR once a year |
| 45 to 59 | allowed | continue; check eGFR every 3 to 6 months |
| 30 to 44 | do not start | reduce to no more than 1000 mg a day; review the benefit |
| below 30 | do not start | stop |

The limit applies to the total daily dose. Metformin 1000 mg twice a day is 2000 mg a day, which is above the 1000 mg limit for an eGFR of 30 to 44.

## Acting on a falling eGFR

A patient whose eGFR has fallen into a lower band while the dose stayed the same has a medication safety flag. The prescribing clinician should adjust the dose within two weeks. A pharmacist medication review can be booked through ops-01. Also review the CKD stage on the problem list (ckd-01), since a falling eGFR often means the coded stage is out of date.

## Temporary pauses

Pause metformin during acute illness with vomiting, diarrhea or dehydration, and before iodinated contrast imaging when eGFR is below 60. Restart when the patient is eating and drinking normally and kidney function is back to baseline.
