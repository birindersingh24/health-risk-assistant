# Prediabetes follow-up

> **Mock document for a software demo. Not clinical guidance; do not use for patient care.**
> Example Clinic Protocols, Primary Care.

## Definition and recording

Prediabetes means an HbA1c of 5.7% to 6.4%, or a fasting plasma glucose of 100 to 125 mg/dL, as defined in dm-01. It is a risk state, not a diagnosis of diabetes. Record it on the problem list as R73.03 so that the yearly follow-up in this protocol is not missed.

Do not describe a patient with prediabetes as diabetic, and do not start a diabetes care plan. A single HbA1c at the top of the range, such as 6.4%, is still prediabetes.

## Monitoring

Repeat HbA1c once a year. Repeat it sooner, after six months, when HbA1c has risen by 0.3 percentage points or more over the past year, or when the patient has gained a lot of weight.

Check blood pressure at every visit and a lipid panel on the normal schedule in scr-01. People with prediabetes often have other cardiovascular risk factors, so calculate the risk score in risk-01 at least once a year.

If any later HbA1c is 6.5% or higher, follow the confirmation steps in dm-01.

## Lifestyle program

Offer every patient with prediabetes a place in the clinic's lifestyle program. The program aims for a weight loss of 5 to 7 percent of body weight and at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking. Referral goes to the diabetes educator (ops-01). Document whether the patient accepted or declined, following ops-02.

## When to consider metformin

Consider metformin, alongside the lifestyle program, for patients with prediabetes who also have any of the following:

- a body mass index of 35 or more,
- age below 60,
- a history of gestational diabetes.

Check kidney function before starting and follow ckd-02 for dosing. Metformin is optional in prediabetes; the decision is shared with the patient.
