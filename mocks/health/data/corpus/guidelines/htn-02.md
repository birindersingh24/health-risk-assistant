# Hypertension treatment and escalation

> **Mock document for a software demo. Not clinical guidance; do not use for patient care.**
> Example Clinic Protocols, Primary Care.

## Targets

For most adults on treatment, the target is below 130/80 mmHg. For patients aged 75 or older, or with frequent falls or dizziness on standing, a systolic target below 140 is acceptable when agreed with the patient.

## Starting treatment

- Stage 1 (htn-01): start one medicine when the risk band is intermediate or high (risk-01), or when the patient has diabetes or chronic kidney disease. Otherwise start with lifestyle changes and recheck in three to six months.
- Stage 2: start one medicine, or two from different classes when the reading is more than 20/10 above target.

First-line classes are ACE inhibitors (such as lisinopril), ARBs (such as losartan), calcium channel blockers (such as amlodipine) and thiazide diuretics (such as hydrochlorothiazide). Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.

## Stepping up

Review four to six weeks after each change. If the patient is not at target, first increase to the full dose, then add a medicine from a different class. Recheck potassium and eGFR within two weeks of starting or increasing an ACE inhibitor, ARB or diuretic.

## Resistant hypertension

Blood pressure is resistant when it stays above target although the patient is taking three medicines from different classes, at full or best-tolerated doses, and one of them is a diuretic.

Before using the label, rule out the common false alarms:

1. Missed doses. Ask directly, check pharmacy refill dates, and read the notes.
2. Measurement error, such as a cuff that is too small or no rest before the reading.
3. White-coat effect. Compare with home readings.
4. Medicines that raise blood pressure, such as regular anti-inflammatory painkillers (NSAIDs) and decongestants.

When these are excluded, the patient has confirmed resistant hypertension. Refer to the hypertension clinic (ops-01) for investigation of secondary causes and advice on a fourth medicine. Do not add a fourth medicine in primary care without that review.
