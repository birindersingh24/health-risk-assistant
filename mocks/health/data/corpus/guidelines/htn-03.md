# Severe blood pressure: urgency and emergency

> **Mock document for a software demo. Not clinical guidance; do not use for patient care.**
> Example Clinic Protocols, Primary Care.

## What counts as severe

A reading of 180 mmHg systolic or higher, or 120 mmHg diastolic or higher, is in the severe range. Either number alone is enough. Repeat the reading after five minutes of quiet rest before acting, but do not delay if the patient has symptoms.

## Emergency: severe reading with signs of organ damage

Ask about and look for signs of acute damage to the heart, brain, kidneys or eyes:

- chest pain or pressure, or shortness of breath,
- new weakness, numbness, confusion or difficulty speaking,
- severe headache with confusion or vomiting,
- new loss or blurring of vision.

If any is present, this is a hypertensive emergency. Send the patient to the emergency department immediately by ambulance (ops-01). Do not try to bring the pressure down in the clinic.

## Urgency: severe reading without organ damage

If the reading stays in the severe range and none of the signs above is present, this is hypertensive urgency. It needs action the same day, but not the emergency department.

1. A clinician reviews the patient the same day.
2. Look for a reason: missed or run-out medicine, recent NSAID or decongestant use, pain or anxiety.
3. Restart missed medicine, or increase or add oral treatment following htn-02.
4. Bring the pressure down gradually over days, not hours. Rapid lowering can cause harm.
5. Book a follow-up within one week.

Document which signs were checked and found absent. An assistant summarizing a severe reading should state whether the notes record symptoms; if the notes are silent, say so rather than assume either way.
